# Candidates — Invent a Word We Need

Judge criteria, most important first: (1) fresh word + one clear line;
(2) fills a real gap the reader recognises at once; (3) definition exact and
self-contained, lands in one reading; (4) sayable, spellable, memorable,
sounds as though English grew it; (5) fresh and surprising.

DeepSeek (100milionseek) was down for the whole build: four brutal-mode
calls (feelings, annoyance, critique — 200-token micro-calls, 3 retries each)
all failed with HTTP 524 / empty replies, so the brainstorm and critique
below are the builder's own, checked against the judge criteria in words.

## The candidates

1. **Planfall** — the quiet relief when a plan you dreaded falls through. (66 chars)
2. **Doorlapse** — forgetting why you entered a room the moment you cross its doorway. (79 chars)
3. **Wavemiss** — the hot-cheeked moment you wave back and realise they were waving at someone else. (88 chars)
4. **Finegap** — the beat before you say "I'm fine" when you are anything but. (70 chars)
5. **Dotdrop** — the small sinking when "typing…" stops and no message ever arrives. (76 chars)
6. **Storyache** — the hollow ache when a story you loved ends and its world closes. (79 chars)
7. **Nodbluff** — nodding along to words you stopped following three sentences ago. (78 chars)
8. **Wordshame** — the sting of learning you have mispronounced a word for years. (74 chars)
9. **Greenrun** — the smug glide of catching every traffic light green in a row. (72 chars)
10. **Youtoo** — the automatic "you too" you say to a farewell that cannot be returned. (78 chars)

## Critique

- **Planfall.** Gap: enormous and universal — everyone has felt guilty relief
  when a dreaded plan collapses; English has words for the dread and the
  cancellation but none for the relief itself. Definition lands in one
  reading ("quiet relief" + "dreaded" do all the work). Word: two plain
  English parts, sayable, spellable, memorable. Feeling-words are the
  coinages dictionaries keep (cf. *schadenfreude*, *saudade*). Weakness:
  "fall" names the plan collapsing as much as the feeling; the definition
  pins it to the relief.
- **Doorlapse.** Gap: enormous — the doorway blank hits everyone weekly.
  Word is clean and the image is instant. Weaker than Planfall on criterion
  2's "felt" side: it names a cognitive glitch, not a feeling, and glitch
  words are less dictionary-worthy than feeling words. Runner-up.
- **Wavemiss.** Gap: universal and funny; the hot-cheeked shame is instantly
  recognisable. Word is transparent. Weaker than Planfall: the moment is a
  small embarrassment, a thinner gap than a recurring emotion, and "miss"
  slightly muddles the meaning (you didn't miss the wave, you mis-aimed it).
- **Finegap.** Short, memorable, real gap. Weaker: the "beat" is a hesitation,
  not quite a thing in itself, and the definition leans on the quoted
  "I'm fine" to carry the meaning.
- **Dotdrop.** Modern, universal among messagers; "dotdrop" is evocative.
  Weaker: the gap belongs to one medium (texting), narrower than Planfall's,
  and the ellipsis in the definition costs clarity.
- **Storyache.** Real gap ("post-book depression" is a known feeling) and the
  *heartache* pattern makes it sound English-grown. Weaker: the ache is
  really just sadness at an ending, which English already names tolerably
  well ("bereft", "wistful"); the gap is thinner.
- **Nodbluff.** Universal, and "bluff" is a sharp word choice. Weaker: the
  behaviour is already covered by "nodding along" / "feigning attention";
  the gap is a relabelling more than a new naming.
- **Wordshame.** Real sting, clean word. Weaker: "shame" already names the
  feeling; the coinage adds a cause, not a new feeling.
- **Greenrun.** Fun, but the gap is a trivial pleasure, and "green run" is
  already a ski-slope term — a real collision. Cut.
- **Youtoo.** Funny and instantly recognisable, but the word is just "you
  too" respelled — a phrase, not a word English could have grown; weakest
  on criterion 4. Cut.

## Pick

**Planfall** — the quiet relief when a plan you dreaded falls through.

It wins on criterion 2 (the widest, most felt gap: a recurring emotion with
no name), criterion 3 (the definition is exact in one reading), and
criterion 4 (two plain English parts, sayable and spellable). Doorlapse is
the runner-up and would be the pick if the judge favoured a cognitive glitch
over a feeling; Wavemiss third for humour and freshness.
